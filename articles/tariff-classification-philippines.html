Understanding Tariff Classification in the Philippines

Apr 3, 2026 · 1 min read

Tariff classification is a fundamental part of importation, as it determines the applicable duties, taxes, and regulatory requirements for imported goods. In the Philippines, classification is based on the Harmonized System (HS), an internationally recognized system of product categorization.

What Is Tariff Classification?

Each product is assigned an HS Code, which corresponds to a specific description and duty rate. Accurate classification is essential, as misclassification can result in incorrect duty assessment, penalties, or delays in customs clearance.

Why It Matters

Getting the classification right affects:

  • Duties and taxes payable
  • Compliance with regulations
  • Risk of penalties and audit findings

Even minor errors can lead to reassessment or delays that disrupt your supply chain.

Common Classification Issues

Importers often run into problems like:

  • Using overly broad classifications
  • Misclassifying similar products
  • Relying on supplier descriptions without independent verification

How to Get It Right

Classification should always be based on material composition, function or use, and technical specifications - not just the product name.

Given how technical tariff classification can be, working with a licensed customs broker is the most reliable way to get it right the first time. Misclassification - even when unintentional - can result in back duties, penalties, and complications during post-clearance audit.
