Luzon International Premiere Airport Development Corp. (LIPAD) has allocated 30 hectares within the Clark Freeport Zone for a new Cargo City development that will support warehousing, logistics, and cold storage operations. The project is a direct response to rising cargo volumes and the continued expansion of global express carriers at Clark International Airport.



## The Numbers Behind the Expansion

Cargo volumes moving through Clark reached 93,900 tons in 2025, while cargo flight movements increased by 40 percent to 6,764 flights over the same period. That growth trajectory, driven in large part by FedEx and UPS scaling up their Pampanga operations, is the primary reason LIPAD is setting aside dedicated land for a purpose-built cargo hub rather than relying on incremental expansion of existing facilities.



## What Cargo City Is Designed to Support

- Warehousing capacity for growing import and export volumes.
- Cold storage facilities for temperature-sensitive cargo.
- General logistics infrastructure to support e-commerce, electronics, and high-value manufacturing shippers.



## Why This Matters Beyond Clark

One of the stated goals of the Cargo City project is to ease congestion at major Philippine ports by giving shippers a viable alternative hub outside Metro Manila. For businesses evaluating [importation costs](/articles/importation-costs-breakdown-philippines) and routing options, an expanded Clark logistics ecosystem, complete with warehousing and cold storage, could become a meaningful factor in future supply chain decisions, particularly for industries like electronics and e-commerce that are expected to be early beneficiaries.

As the Cargo City development moves from land allocation to actual construction, expect more detail on timelines, leasing terms, and which logistics operators will anchor the new facilities.
