The Bureau of Customs (BOC) has reorganized part of its internal structure as part of its wider modernization program in 2026, creating a dedicated Project Management Office (PMO) and reshaping its former PMO into the new Customs Policy and Issuance Office. For Philippine importers and the brokers who represent them, these changes signal a push toward faster, more consistent policymaking, and it is worth understanding what each office now handles.



## Two Offices, Two Distinct Roles

The newly created Project Management Office takes over the planning, implementation, and monitoring of BOC's major initiatives, from digitalization projects to trade facilitation programs. Meanwhile, the reorganized Customs Policy and Issuance Office is now responsible for policy formulation, the issuance of new customs administrative orders, and the dissemination of information to stakeholders.

Splitting these functions is meant to prevent the bottlenecks that come from a single office trying to both write policy and manage large-scale projects at the same time. It also gives brokers and importers a clearer point of reference when trying to track where a particular [BOC accreditation](/articles/boc-registration-accreditation-philippines) rule or a new customs administrative order originated.



## What This Means for Businesses Working With BOC

In the long term, BOC expects the restructuring to streamline trade facilitation, improve regulatory transparency, and support a more competitive Philippine trade environment. In practical terms, this should translate into:

- More consistent and predictable customs policy issuances, rather than ad hoc directives.
- Clearer accountability for major modernization projects, including digital initiatives.
- Improved communication from BOC when new circulars or orders are released.



## Staying Ahead of the Changes

Reorganizations like this one are also a reminder that customs compliance in the Philippines is not static. Importers who work with a [licensed customs broker](/articles/role-of-customs-broker-philippines) are generally better positioned to keep up with new issuances as they come out, since brokers actively monitor BOC circulars as part of the [customs clearance process](/articles/customs-clearance-process-in-the-philippines-explained).

As BOC continues to formalize its Integrity, Accountability, and Modernization (IAM) agenda, expect more organizational and process changes in the coming months. Businesses that stay engaged with these updates, rather than reacting to them after the fact, will have an easier time adjusting their import operations accordingly.
