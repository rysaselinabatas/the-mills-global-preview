The Bureau of Customs (BOC) has seized agricultural goods valued at more than PHP7.7 million after discovering misdeclared shipments inside four container vans at the Port of Manila. The case is a reminder of how strictly BOC enforces declaration accuracy, particularly for agricultural imports.



## What Happened

Customs examiners found 12,909 boxes of fresh carrots and 1,452 boxes of egg noodles concealed inside cargo that had been declared as assorted food products. The shipments, which arrived in March 2026, were flagged after intelligence information prompted BOC to issue alert orders. Subsequent inspection confirmed the misdeclaration, leading to Warrants of Seizure and Detention and the eventual forfeiture of the goods in favor of the government.



## Why Declaration Accuracy Matters

This case illustrates the practical consequences of inaccurate [goods declaration](/articles/goods-declaration-philippines), even when the underlying products are not prohibited. Misdeclaring the specific nature or classification of agricultural cargo, whether intentional or the result of poor documentation practices, can trigger [penalties and seizures](/articles/penalties-seizures-customs-philippines) that go well beyond the cost of correcting the declaration.

- Alert orders can be triggered by intelligence information, not just routine inspection.
- Misdeclaration findings can lead to full forfeiture of the goods, not just fines.
- Agricultural shipments face additional scrutiny under anti-smuggling laws.



## Lessons for Importers

BOC Commissioner Ariel Nepomuceno reaffirmed the agency's commitment to strengthening enforcement against smuggling, particularly where it affects local farmers and consumers. For legitimate importers, the takeaway is straightforward: declarations need to accurately reflect the actual contents of a shipment down to the product level. Working with an experienced broker through the full [importation process](/articles/step-by-step-importation-guide-philippines) significantly reduces the risk of documentation errors that could be mistaken for, or actually constitute, misdeclaration.
