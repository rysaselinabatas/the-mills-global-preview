Trade compliance risk management is essential for businesses engaged in importing and exporting goods because it helps ensure adherence to customs regulations and international trade laws. Without proper compliance, companies may face penalties, shipment delays, or reputational damage that can disrupt operations. As global trade becomes more complex, organizations must take a structured and proactive approach to managing regulatory obligations. A strong compliance system not only reduces risk but also improves efficiency in cross-border transactions.



One of the most important risk areas is tariff classification, where goods must be correctly assigned the appropriate customs codes. Errors in classification can lead to incorrect duty payments, customs audits, or legal penalties. Another major concern involves valuation accuracy and failure to secure required permits, which can result in shipments being held or rejected at the border. These issues are often linked to common compliance mistakes, such as using outdated regulations, inconsistent documentation, or lack of proper internal review before submitting customs declarations.



To reduce these risks, businesses must establish strong internal controls that guide every step of the import process. This includes standardized procedures for documentation, approval workflows, and centralized recordkeeping systems to ensure accuracy and consistency. Employee training is also essential so that staff understand evolving trade regulations and avoid errors that could lead to compliance violations. Regular monitoring of regulatory updates helps organizations stay aligned with current legal requirements across different jurisdictions.



Periodic audits and compliance reviews are also important tools for identifying weaknesses in existing systems and preventing future violations. These evaluations help businesses detect errors in classification, documentation gaps, or missing permits before they become serious issues. Many companies also rely on advisory services to gain expert guidance on customs rules, risk assessment, and compliance strategy. By combining internal controls, audits, and expert support, businesses can build a proactive compliance framework that ensures operational stability and protects against regulatory exposure.



## Compliance Mistakes



To effectively manage compliance risks, they need a clear process for identifying and preventing common mistakes.



**WHAT TO DO**



- Review Import Documentation Before SubmissionThey should verify if the commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading are consistent in:
- Product description
- Quantity
- Value
- Validate Supplier Information



They must ensure that the supplier provides accurate and complete details. Request standardized templates to avoid inconsistencies.



- Conduct a Pre-Entry Check



Before lodging the import entry, they should:



- Confirm HS code classification
- Check if permits are required
- Validate declared customs value
- Implement a Double-Check System Assign a second reviewer or compliance officer to check all declarations before submission.
- Perform Regular Internal ReviewsThey should periodically review past transactions to identify recurring errors and correct them.



## [Tariff Classification](/articles/understanding-tariff-classification-in-the-philippines)



To reduce the risk of misclassification, they need a structured approach to assigning HS codes.



**WHAT TO DO**



- Gather Complete Product Information They should collect:
- Product composition
- Function and use
- Technical specifications
- Packaging details
- Identify the Correct HS Code Use the Philippine Tariff system and:
- Start with the correct chapter
- Narrow down to heading and subheading
- Apply General Rules for Interpretation (GRI)
- Cross-Check Similar Classifications They should compare the classification with:
- Previous import entries
- Customs rulings (if available)
- Assess Duty and Tax Impact Confirm that the classification aligns with the correct duty rate and taxes.
- Document Your Basis for Classification Keep records explaining why they selected the HS code. This is critical during audits.
- Seek Expert Review When Needed If the product is complex, they should consult a customs broker or specialist.
