The importation of automotive units and industrial machinery into the Philippines is subject to regulatory requirements depending on the nature and use of the equipment. Certain types of machinery may require permits from the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) or other regulatory bodies, making compliance a critical first step in the process.



Customs clearance requires accurate tariff classification, as machinery often falls under complex tariff codes. Proper valuation is also essential due to the high value of such equipment, directly affecting applicable duties and taxes and overall landed cost.



Importers must prepare complete import documentation, including commercial invoices, packing lists, and certificates of origin when applicable. Ensuring accuracy and completeness of documents helps avoid delays and penalties during customs processing.



Common issues include incorrect classification, undervaluation, and failure to secure required permits. These challenges can significantly impact timelines and increase costs if not addressed properly.



Professional advisory support and reliable trucking services are often necessary to manage the complexities associated with machinery importation, from customs clearance to final delivery.



## [Tariff Classification](/articles/understanding-tariff-classification-in-the-philippines)



Tariff classification is the process of assigning the correct Harmonized System (HS) code to imported machinery and automotive equipment. This determines the applicable duty rate, taxes, and regulatory requirements. Machinery often falls under Chapters 84 and 87 of the HS Code, making classification highly technical and critical for compliance.



**Agencies**



- Bureau of Customs (BOC) - Responsible for classification rulings and enforcement
- World Customs Organization (WCO) - Maintains the Harmonized System framework
- Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) - May issue guidance for regulated goods



**Permits**



- Advance Ruling on Tariff Classification (optional but recommended for high-value machinery)
- Product-specific clearances (if classification triggers regulation)



How to Ensure Correct Tariff Classification?



A quick guide to verifying the correct HS code for machinery, ensuring accurate duty rates and compliance with customs regulations.



**1.Identify correct HS Code (6–10 digit level)**



Determine the precise HS code that matches the machinery’s function, specifications, and components to ensure proper classification under customs rules.



**2.Review explanatory notes and rulings**



Examine official classification guidelines and prior rulings to validate the selected HS code and avoid misinterpretation.



**3.Confirm duty rate and VAT implications**



Check the applicable duty rate and corresponding VAT based on the assigned HS code to accurately estimate import costs.



**4.Secure advance ruling if classification is unclear**



Request an official classification decision from the Bureau of Customs when there is uncertainty, especially for complex or high-value machinery.



**5.Ensure consistency across all shipping documents**



Verify that the HS code and product description are uniform across invoices, packing lists, and declarations to prevent delays or penalties.



## [Import Documentation](/articles/import-documentation-checklist)



Import documentation ensures that goods are legally declared and traceable. For machinery importation, documentation must be complete and accurate due to the high value and regulatory scrutiny involved.



**Agencies involved:**



- Bureau of Customs (BOC) - Reviews and processes import entries
- Department of Finance (DOF) - Oversees customs policies
- Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) - Facilitates port operations



**Required Permits:**



- Import Entry Declaration (Single Administrative Document - SAD)
- Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
- Commercial Invoice and Packing List
- Certificate of Origin (for preferential tariff treatment)
- Import permits (if regulated machinery)



**Required Documents for Customs Clearance**



A summary of essential documents needed to process and release imported machinery through customs without delays.



**1.Verify completeness of commercial invoice (value, description, terms)**



Ensure the invoice reflects accurate pricing, detailed product descriptions, and correct trade terms (e.g., CIF, FOB) to support proper valuation and customs assessment.



**2.Ensure packing list matches shipment contents**



Check that quantities, weights, and packaging details align with the actual cargo to avoid discrepancies during inspection and clearance.



**3.Confirm Bill of Lading details (consignee, ports, cargo description)**



Review the Bill of Lading to ensure all shipment information is correct, including consignee name, port of loading/discharge, and cargo details, as errors may cause delays.



**4.Attach certificates (origin, compliance, or inspection if required)**



Provide all necessary supporting documents such as Certificate of Origin or inspection certificates to meet regulatory and preferential tariff requirements.



**5.Encode and lodge declaration through BOC system**



Accurately input shipment data into the Bureau of Customs electronic system to formally declare the goods and initiate the clearance process



## [Duties and Taxes](/articles/import-duties-and-taxes-in-the-philippines-explained)



Duties and taxes are calculated based on the customs value, classification, and applicable trade agreements. Machinery imports typically involve customs duty and Value-Added Tax (VAT), with possible exemptions depending on use and incentives.



**Agencies**



- Bureau of Customs (BOC) - Assesses duties and taxes
- Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) - Administers VAT
- Department of Finance (DOF) - Sets fiscal policies



**Permits**



- Certificate of Origin (to avail reduced tariffs under trade agreements such as ASEAN)
- Tax exemption certificates (if applicable under investment incentives)
- Authority to Release Imported Goods (after payment)



**Steps in Computing Duties and Taxes**



**1.Determine customs value (CIF: Cost + Insurance + Freight)**



Establish the total landed value of the machinery by combining cost, insurance, and freight, which serves as the basis for duty and tax computation.



**2.Apply correct duty rate based on HS Code**



Use the assigned HS code to identify the applicable tariff rate, ensuring accurate calculation of import duties.



**3.Compute VAT (typically 12% in the Philippines)**



Calculate Value-Added Tax based on the total customs value plus duties and other charges, in line with Bureau of Internal Revenue regulations.



**4.Check eligibility for preferential tariffs or exemptions**



Review if the shipment qualifies for reduced or zero tariffs under trade agreements or investment incentives, supported by proper documentation.



**5.Pay assessed duties and taxes prior to release**



Settle all computed charges with the Bureau of Customs to secure the release of goods from customs custody.



## [Trucking Services](/services/trucking)



Trucking services handle the inland transportation of imported machinery from ports to warehouses or project sites. Due to the size and weight of industrial machinery, specialized transport solutions are often required.



What are the agencies involved?



- Land Transportation Office (LTO) - Regulates vehicle registration and roadworthiness
- Department of Transportation (DOTr) - Oversees transport policies
- Philippine Ports Authority (PPA) - Coordinates port logistics



**Required Permits**



- Special permits for oversized or overweight cargo
- Delivery permits from port authorities
- Gate passes and clearance documents from customs



**Arranging Transport from Port to Final Destination**



**1.Select appropriate truck type (flatbed, low-bed, heavy haul)**



Choose the right vehicle based on the size, weight, and configuration of the machinery.



- Flatbed: standard equipment and containerized cargo
- Low-bed: tall or heavy machinery (e.g., excavators, industrial units)
- Heavy haul: oversized or abnormal loads requiring special handling



**2.Secure permits for oversized machinery**



Obtain required transport permits if cargo exceeds legal size or weight limits.



- Apply for special permits from the Land Transportation Office
- Coordinate with local authorities for route approval if needed
- Ensure compliance with axle load and dimension restrictions



**3.Coordinate port pickup schedule**



Align trucking arrangements with port release timelines to avoid storage or demurrage charges.



- Confirm cargo availability and customs clearance status
- Book trucking in advance, especially for heavy equipment
- Coordinate with port operators and brokers for smooth release



**4.Ensure cargo is properly secured and insured**



Protect machinery during transit to prevent damage or loss.



- Use proper lashing, bracing, and securing equipment
- Conduct pre-departure inspection of cargo positioning
- Arrange cargo insurance coverage for high-value equipment



**5.Plan route considering road restrictions and clearances**



Map out the safest and most efficient delivery route.



- Check bridge load limits, road width, and height clearances
- Avoid restricted zones or roads not suitable for heavy transport
- Consider traffic conditions and travel time windows
