The importation of agricultural products into the Philippines is subject to stringent regulation to safeguard public health, food security, and local agricultural industries. The Department of Agriculture (DA), through its various bureaus, exercises primary jurisdiction over the importation of such goods.



Importers are required to secure sanitary and phytosanitary import permits prior to shipment. These permits certify that the goods comply with health and safety standards and are not likely to introduce pests or diseases into the country. In addition, certain products are subject to quarantine inspection upon arrival.



From a customs perspective, agricultural shipments undergo the standard clearance process, including declaration, classification, and assessment of duties and taxes. However, clearance is contingent upon compliance with DA requirements, and failure to secure the necessary permits may result in seizure or rejection of the shipment.



Common challenges in agricultural importation include delays in permit processing, non-compliance with quarantine standards, and discrepancies in documentation.



Importers must exercise due diligence in securing all required approvals prior to shipment and ensure coordination between regulatory compliance and customs clearance procedures.

## [Documentation Checklist](/articles/import-documentation-checklist)



Complete and accurate documentation is essential for both regulatory approval and customs clearance.



**Agencies Involved**



- Bureau of Customs - Reviews and validates import documents
- Department of Agriculture - Confirms permit-related documentation



**Required Documents**



- Commercial Invoice - Establishes value of goods
- Packing List - Provides shipment breakdown
- Bill of Lading - Confirms transport details
- Import Permit (SPSIC) - Regulatory approval for importation
- Certificate of Origin (if applicable) - Determines eligibility for tariff preferences



**WHAT TO DO**



- Prepare documents before shipment arrival
- Complete all required documents before the cargo arrives
- Helps avoid port storage charges and delays
- Ensures faster processing during customs clearance
- Ensure consistency across all documents
- Match product description, quantity, and value across all documents
- Verify consignee and shipment details are identical
- Prevents discrepancies that may trigger inspection or penalties
- Submit documents to customs and DA
- Provide documents to the Bureau of Customs for clearance processing
- Submit required permits and supporting documents to the Department of Agriculture
- Confirms compliance with both customs and regulatory requirements
- Keep copies for audit and compliance
- Retain copies of all import documents for record-keeping
- Useful for audits, disputes, or post-entry verification
- Ensures traceability and long-term compliance
