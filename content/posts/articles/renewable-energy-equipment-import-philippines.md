Renewable energy equipment plays a vital role in supporting sustainable development and reducing dependence on fossil fuels. In the Philippines, the government actively promotes the use of clean energy technologies such as solar panels, wind turbines, inverters, and battery storage systems. For importers, understanding the customs duties, taxes, and regulatory requirements is essential to ensure compliance and maximize cost efficiency.



**Types of Renewable Energy Equipment**



Commonly imported renewable energy equipment includes:



- Solar panels and photovoltaic (PV) modules
- Solar inverters and charge controllers
- Wind turbines and components
- Battery energy storage systems
- Hydropower generators and parts



These products are widely used in residential, commercial, and industrial applications to support energy independence and sustainability goals.



**Duties and Taxes on Renewable Energy Equipment**



Import duties for renewable energy equipment in the Philippines typically range from 0% to 5%, depending on the product classification and applicable tariff codes. In many cases, solar panels and certain components may qualify for zero-duty rates under government incentives or international agreements.



However, imports are still subject to:



- 12% Value-Added Tax (VAT) based on the total landed cost
- Other charges such as brokerage fees, arrastre, and wharfage



The landed cost formula includes:



Customs Value (CIF) + Duties + Import Charges = VAT Base



Government Incentives and Benefits



The Philippine government encourages renewable energy investments through incentives such as:



- Duty-free importation for qualified projects
- VAT zero-rating for certain renewable energy sales
- Income tax holidays for registered energy developers



To access these benefits, importers must register with relevant agencies such as the Department of Energy (DOE) or the Board of Investments (BOI).



**Regulatory Requirements**



While many renewable energy products are not heavily restricted, importers must still comply with:



- Proper tariff classification (HS Code)
- Accurate commercial documentation (invoice, packing list, bill of lading)
- Product standards and certifications (if applicable)



Certain electrical components may require compliance with Philippine safety standards before distribution.



**Cost-Saving Strategies**



To reduce import costs, consider the following:



- Utilize Free Trade Agreements (FTAs) for preferential duty rates
- Ensure correct HS code classification to avoid overpayment
- Consolidate shipments to optimize freight costs
- Work with licensed customs brokers for smooth clearance



Strategic planning can significantly lower total landed costs and improve project profitability.



## [Customs Clearance Process in the Philippines](/articles/customs-clearance-process-in-the-philippines-explained)



Customs Clearance Process of Renewable Energy Equipment in the Philippines



Customs clearance for renewable energy (RE) equipment in the Philippines allows duty-free importation under Renewable Energy Act of 2008 (RA 9513), provided the importer is a qualified RE developer and complies with requirements from the Department of Energy Philippines and Bureau of Customs.



**Key Regulatory Requirements**



- Eligible Importers: Only registered RE developers with a Certificate of Registration (COR)
- DOE Endorsement: Must be secured before importation
- Direct Utilization: Equipment must be used exclusively for RE projects
- Required Documents:
- Bill of Lading / Airway Bill
- Commercial Invoice
- Packing List
- DOE Certificate of Registration (COR)
- Approved Work and Financial Program (WFP)



**Customs Clearance Process**



- Registration - Obtain COR from DOE
- DOE Endorsement - Confirm eligibility and non-availability locally
- Shipment Documentation - Ensure documents are under the RE developer’s name
- Import Entry Filing - Submit entry to BOC (E2M system)
- Assessment & Examination - Customs verifies compliance
- Duty-Free Availment - Apply 0% duty under RA 9513
- Cargo Release - Issuance of Authority to Release Imported Goods (ATRIG)



**Key Incentives (RA 9513)**



- 0% Import Duty on RE equipment (up to 10 years)
- VAT Incentives for qualified transactions
- Tax Benefits for registered RE developers



Restriction: If equipment is sold or transferred within 10 years to a non-RE entity, duties and taxes must be paid.



**Common Issues**



- Delays due to missing DOE endorsement or incomplete documents
- Incorrect consignee details in shipping documents
- Misuse of incentives leading to penalties and tax liabilities



## [Import Duties and Taxes Philippines](/articles/import-duties-and-taxes-in-the-philippines-explained)



**Duties and VAT on Renewable Energy Equipment in the Philippines**



Renewable energy (RE) equipment imported into the Philippines may qualify for duty-free and 0% VAT incentives if the importer is a registered RE developer. These benefits are governed by the Renewable Energy Act of 2008 (RA 9513) and further clarified under CREATE MORE Act (RA 12066).



**Import Duties**



- 0% Import Duty for up to 10 years for registered RE developers
- Applies to machinery, equipment, and materials used in RE projects



**Conditions:**



- Must be directly and exclusively used for RE facilities (e.g., solar panels, inverters, batteries)
- Must not be locally available in sufficient quantity/quality at competitive prices



**Transfer Restriction:**



If equipment is sold or transferred to a non-RE entity within 10 years, duties and taxes become payable based on net book value.



**Value-Added Tax (VAT)**



- 0% VAT on Importation of RE equipment directly used in registered projects
- 0% VAT on Local Purchases of goods and services for RE development



**Important Update:**



A ruling by the Supreme Court of the Philippines confirms that DOE endorsement is NOT required for VAT zero-rating - a valid COR is sufficient.



**Standard Requirements for Clearance**



To avail of incentives with the Bureau of Customs, importers must submit:



- DOE Certificate of Registration (COR) - proves RE developer status
- DOE Certificate of Endorsement (COE) - required for duty-free importation
- BOI Registration - for full fiscal incentives
- Shipping Documents - Invoice, Packing List, Bill of Lading/AWB
- BPS/ICC Clearance - for regulated electrical components



**Summary of Major Taxes**



For registered renewable energy developers under Renewable Energy Act of 2008 (RA 9513), the major taxes are as follows:



- Customs Duty: Set at 0% during the first 10 years from registration, provided the equipment is directly used for the renewable energy project.
- Value-Added Tax (VAT): Also 0%, as long as the importation or purchase is directly attributable to the registered renewable energy project.
- Corporate Income Tax: Imposed at 10%, but only after the 7-year Income Tax Holiday (ITH) period has ended.
- Tariff Classification Guide Philippines



**Tariff Classification of Renewable Energy Equipment in the Philippines**



In the Philippines, renewable energy (RE) equipment is classified under the ASEAN Harmonized Tariff Nomenclature (AHTN) 2022. Even if registered RE developers enjoy 0% duty under Renewable Energy Act of 2008 (RA 9513), proper tariff classification is still required to avoid delays, penalties, or incorrect tax assessment by the Bureau of Customs.



**Common RE Equipment Classifications**



- Solar Panels (Photovoltaic)
- Heading 85.41
- AHTN 8541.42.00 / 8541.43.00 (PV cells and modules)
- Inverters (Static Converters)
- Heading 85.04
- AHTN 8504.40.xx (solar inverters and converters)
- Energy Storage (Batteries)
- Heading 85.07
- AHTN 8507.60.90 (lithium-ion storage systems)
- Wind Turbines
- Heading 85.02
- AHTN 8502.31.00 (wind-powered generating sets)
- Geothermal / Power Generating Sets
- AHTN 8502.39.32 (complete generating systems)
- Parts and Accessories (BOS)
- Heading 85.37 / 85.38 depending on function (e.g., control panels)



**Classification Tools and Verification**



To ensure accurate classification:



- Philippine Tariff Finder (PTF) - Use the online tool of the Tariff Commission Philippines to search AHTN codes
- Advance Ruling - Apply through TCIS-OAS for binding classification (valid for 5 years)
- Technical Review - Check product specifications to match correct headings



**Key Reminder for Duty-Free Availment**



Correct classification alone is not enough. To apply the 0% duty rate, importers must:



- Use the correct Procedure Code in the BOC E2M system
- Link the shipment to the DOE endorsement
