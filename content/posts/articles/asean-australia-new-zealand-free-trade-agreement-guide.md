The ASEAN-Australia-New Zealand Free Trade Area (AANZFTA) strengthens economic partnerships and serves as a building block toward regional economic integration. The agreement progressively liberalizes trade in goods and services while facilitating investment opportunities among the participating parties. Philippine importers can use AANZFTA to reduce [import duties and taxes](/articles/import-duties-taxes-philippines) on eligible goods from Australia and New Zealand.



## Objectives of AANZFTA

- Eliminate or reduce tariffs and non-tariff barriers
- Liberalize trade in goods and services
- Promote and protect investments
- Strengthen economic cooperation among members
- Provide special treatment for developing ASEAN countries



## Rules of Origin (ROO)

The agreement treats a good as an originating good if a party wholly produces or obtains it, or if it satisfies specific requirements for goods not wholly produced:

- **Regional Value Content (RVC)** - A party considers a good originating if it possesses a regional value content of at least 40% of the FOB value.
- **Change in Tariff Classification (CTC)** - Parties grant origin if all non-originating materials undergo a change in tariff classification at the four-digit level.
- **De Minimis** - Parties still consider goods as originating if they do not satisfy CTC requirements, provided the value of non-originating materials does not exceed 10% of the FOB value.

For a broader explanation of Rules of Origin across Philippine trade agreements, see our [Rules of Origin Guide](/articles/rules-of-origin-international-trade-law).



## Certificate of Origin

The Certificate of Origin (CO) is the most important document required to claim preferential tariff rates under AANZFTA. It proves that the goods are produced in a member country and meet Rules of Origin requirements. Government-authorized agencies in the exporting country issue the CO. The CO is valid for 12 months from date of issuance.



## How to Avail Preferential Rates

1. **Check Product Eligibility** - Ensure goods meet Rules of Origin
2. **Apply for Certificate of Origin** - Submit documents to authorized issuing body and provide proof of origin
3. **Prepare Documents** - Commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, Certificate of Origin. See our full [Import Documentation Checklist](/articles/import-documentation-checklist-philippines).
4. **Submit to Customs** - Importer submits documents at entry point
5. **Customs Verification** - Customs checks CO and origin, then applies reduced or zero tariff
