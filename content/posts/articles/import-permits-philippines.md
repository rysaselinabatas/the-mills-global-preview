Not all goods can be freely imported into the Philippines. Depending on the nature of the product, importers may be required to secure permits, clearances, or licenses from specific government agencies before their shipment can be processed by the Bureau of Customs.



Knowing which agency has jurisdiction over your goods - and when permits are required - is a critical part of import planning. Issues related to permits are often only discovered when the shipment is already at the port, which leads to delays and additional costs.



## Why Import Permits Are Required

Import permits are issued to regulate goods that may:

- Affect public health and safety
- Impact agriculture and food security
- Require compliance with national standards
- Fall under controlled or sensitive categories

Customs will not release regulated goods without the appropriate documentation. See our [Import Documentation Checklist](/articles/import-documentation-checklist-philippines) for what to prepare.



## Key Regulatory Agencies

### Food and Drug Administration (FDA)

The FDA regulates products with a direct impact on public health, including:

- Processed food products
- Pharmaceuticals
- Medical devices
- Cosmetics

Importers of FDA-regulated goods typically need to secure product registration and/or import permits before shipment arrival.



### Department of Agriculture (DA)

The DA oversees the importation of:

- Meat and poultry products
- Plant and plant-based goods
- Agricultural commodities

Permits are issued to ensure compliance with quarantine, sanitary, and phytosanitary requirements.



### Department of Trade and Industry (DTI)

The DTI regulates certain consumer products and enforces compliance with product standards and safety requirements, including:

- Electrical products
- Construction materials
- Consumer goods subject to Philippine National Standards (PNS)



Other agencies involved in import regulation include the Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR), the Philippine Nuclear Research Institute (PNRI), and the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP), depending on the nature of the goods.



Securing permits before your shipment departs the origin country is always the safer approach. Review our [Importation Timelines and Delays Philippines](/articles/importation-timelines-delays-philippines) guide to understand why timing matters. Last-minute applications after arrival often result in storage charges and processing delays.
