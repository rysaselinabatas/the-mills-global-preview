Bringing home a loved one who passed away is a deeply personal and difficult time. To help families during this period, the Philippine government allows the entry of remains and certain belongings without charging the usual [import duties and taxes](/articles/import-duties-taxes-philippines). This special rule honors the deceased by making the return process more affordable and less complicated for the grieving family.



## Conditions for the Tax Exemption

- The shipment must consist of coffins or urns that contain human remains, bones, or ashes.
- The items can include used personal and household effects that belonged to the person who died.
- The items must be for personal use only and not for sale (not merchandise).
- Vehicles of any type are strictly excluded.
- The total FCA value of the goods must not exceed P150,000 (reviewed every three years).
- All items must be clearly identified and proven to belong to the deceased.



## Required Documents

The Bureau of Customs requires the following paperwork to grant the tax-free status:

1. Import Bill of Lading or Airway Bill - tracks the transport of the shipment
2. Death Certificate - the primary proof of the situation
3. Gate Pass - allows the items to leave the customs area
4. [Goods Declaration](/articles/goods-declaration-philippines) - necessary if the shipment includes personal effects along with the remains

A crucial part of this process involves an affidavit from someone who knows the facts, confirming that the used household goods belonged to the person who passed away.



## Step-by-Step Import Guide



### 1. Gather Official Documents

The representative collects the original Death Certificate and shipping documents like the Airway Bill.



### 2. Prepare the Affidavit

A person with direct knowledge of the deceased signs a sworn statement confirming that the personal effects are used and belonged to the person who died.



### 3. Submit to Customs

The representative files the [Goods Declaration](/articles/goods-declaration-philippines) and all supporting papers with the Bureau of Customs. They must ensure the total value stays under the P150,000 limit.



### 4. Verification and Identification

Customs officers check the shipment to confirm it does not contain vehicles or goods meant for sale.



### 5. Release of Remains and Effects

Once the officers are satisfied that all conditions are met, they issue a Gate Pass. The family then takes the coffin, urn, and belongings home without paying [duties or taxes](/articles/import-duties-taxes-philippines).
