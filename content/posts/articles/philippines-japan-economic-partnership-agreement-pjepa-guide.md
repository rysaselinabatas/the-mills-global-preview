The Philippines-Japan Economic Partnership Agreement (PJEPA) is the first bilateral free trade agreement entered into by the Philippines. It was signed on 09 September 2006, ratified on 08 October 2008, and officially implemented on 11 December 2008. The agreement was designed to strengthen economic ties between the Philippines and Japan by reducing trade barriers and expanding cooperation in multiple sectors.



Unlike traditional free trade agreements that mainly focus on tariff reductions, the PJEPA is considered a new-generation economic partnership agreement because it also covers trade in services, movement of natural persons, investments, intellectual property, environmental cooperation, and competition policy. Philippine importers sourcing Japanese goods may also benefit from the broader regional framework under [AJCEPA](/articles/asean-japan-comprehensive-economic-partnership-agreement-ajcepa), and should compare rates to determine which agreement provides greater benefit.



## Objectives of the PJEPA

- Liberalize and facilitate trade in goods and services between the Philippines and Japan
- Increase investment opportunities and protect investments in both countries
- Promote transparency in government procurement
- Strengthen intellectual property protection
- Address anti-competitive business practices
- Improve the business environment for investors and traders
- Establish dispute settlement procedures
- Promote cooperation in economic and technical fields



## Product Coverage Under PJEPA

The PJEPA covers nearly all products under HS Chapters 1 to 97, except rice and salt. This means most agricultural, industrial, and manufactured goods may qualify for preferential tariff treatment when traded between the two countries. The implementation period for tariff commitments is 10 years from the date the agreement entered into force. During this period, tariffs were reduced or eliminated based on agreed schedules.



## Philippine Tariff Commitments

Under the Philippine offer list, tariff treatment includes immediate tariff elimination for many product lines, gradual reductions over 4, 5, 7, or 10 years, some products subject to special tariff treatment, and certain sensitive items reserved for renegotiation or exclusion. This structure allows industries time to adjust while still promoting free trade. ## Rules of Origin (ROO) Under PJEPA

To qualify for preferential tariffs, products must meet the Rules of Origin. A product may be considered originating if it is wholly obtained or produced in the exporting country, made entirely from originating materials, or satisfies the Product Specific Rules (PSRs) such as tariff classification change or required processing.

The required proof of origin is the Certificate of Origin Form JP. Without this document, customs may deny preferential tariff treatment. ## Why PJEPA Matters to Businesses

For Philippine exporters, PJEPA opens access to one of the world's largest markets with lower tariffs and better trade conditions. For importers, it can reduce costs on eligible Japanese products and machinery. Businesses engaged in automotive parts, electronics, agriculture, manufacturing, and industrial goods can benefit significantly from the agreement when proper [compliance requirements](/articles/import-documentation-checklist-philippines) are met.
