Importers in the Philippines frequently run into compliance issues due to errors in documentation, classification, and regulatory requirements. These mistakes can lead to delays, added costs, and legal consequences.



## Incorrect Tariff Classification

Using the wrong [HS Code](/articles/hs-code-classification-guide-philippines) results in improper duty assessment and can trigger customs audits or penalties. Classification must be based on material composition, function, and technical specifications - not just the product name.



## Undervaluation or Misdeclaration of Goods

Declaring a lower value or inaccurate description of goods may result in reassessment, fines, shipment seizure, or blacklisting. Always declare the true transaction value and provide complete supporting documents.



## Failure to Secure Required Permits

A frequent problem, particularly for controlled goods like food, pharmaceuticals, and electronics. Permits must be secured before shipment arrival - not after goods have reached the port.



## Incomplete or Inconsistent Documentation

Mismatches across the commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading - see our [Import Documentation Checklist](/articles/import-documentation-checklist-philippines) to make sure everything is aligned cause customs to flag shipments for review. All documents must align in value, quantity, and description.



## Non-Compliance Risks

Failing to meet regulatory requirements may result in serious consequences. See our article on [Penalties and Seizures in Philippine Customs](/articles/penalties-seizures-customs-philippines) for what to expect:

- Fines and penalties
- Shipment seizure or forfeiture
- Blacklisting from customs systems
- Delays in shipment release



Getting documentation right and staying on top of regulatory requirements takes effort, but the cost of getting it wrong is far higher. Professional assistance can help identify risks early and keep your shipments compliant.
