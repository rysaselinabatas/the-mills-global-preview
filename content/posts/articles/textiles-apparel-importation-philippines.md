The importation of textiles and apparel into the Philippines is subject to regulatory requirements that vary depending on the type of product, its composition, and its intended use. The Bureau of Customs (BOC) governs the customs clearance process, while the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) may impose additional standards for certain textile products entering the domestic market.



Accurate tariff classification is essential in textiles and apparel importation. Products are classified under Chapters 50 to 63 of the Harmonized System, which cover natural fibers, synthetic materials, woven and knitted fabrics, and finished garments. The classification determines the applicable duty rate and any regulatory requirements, making it a critical step before shipment.



From a documentation standpoint, importers must prepare a complete set of shipping documents including the commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading or airway bill. For shipments originating from countries with which the Philippines has a free trade agreement, a Certificate of Origin may be required to avail of preferential tariff rates.



Certain textile and apparel products may also be subject to quality or safety standards enforced by the DTI. Importers should verify whether the goods fall under mandatory product standards before the shipment departs. Non-compliance can result in the denial of entry or confiscation of goods at the port.



Common compliance issues in this sector include incorrect classification of fabric types, undervaluation of garments, and failure to secure applicable permits or certifications. These can lead to shipment delays, reassessment of duties, or penalties.



Importers are advised to work with licensed customs brokers who are familiar with textile and apparel classification to ensure accurate declarations and smooth clearance. Proper planning and documentation from the outset significantly reduces the risk of delays and additional costs.
