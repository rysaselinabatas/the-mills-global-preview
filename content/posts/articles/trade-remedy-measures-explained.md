Trade remedy measures are legal instruments used by governments to protect domestic industries from unfair trade practices or sudden increases in imports that cause unfair import competition. These measures are recognized under the rules of the World Trade Organization (WTO) and are widely used by countries, including the Philippines, to maintain fair competition and support local producers. The Bureau of Customs (BOC) collects the resulting duties.



For importers, trade remedy measures can affect [import duties and taxes](/articles/import-duties-taxes-philippines) in addition to standard tariff rates. Understanding when these measures are applied helps businesses plan their landed costs accurately.



## What Are Trade Remedy Measures?

Trade remedy measures are policy tools imposed after a formal investigation determines that domestic industries are being harmed by specific import practices. The three primary types are:

**1. Anti-Dumping Measures**

Anti-dumping duties are applied when foreign exporters sell goods in another country at prices lower than their normal value, often below the price charged in their home market. This practice can damage local producers who cannot compete with artificially low prices.

**What is dumping?**

Dumping is a form of unfair trade where an exporter sells goods in a foreign market at prices lower than in its home market or below production cost, causing harm or threatening domestic industries in the importing country. If dumping is proven and injury to domestic industry is established, the importing country may impose additional duties.

**2. Subsidies and Countervailing Measures**

Countervailing duties are used when imported goods benefit from subsidies granted by the exporting country's government. These subsidies may allow exporters to sell products at unfairly low prices. When investigations confirm subsidization and injury to local industries, countervailing duties may be imposed to offset the unfair advantage.

**3. Safeguard Measures**

Safeguard measures are temporary restrictions applied when a sudden surge in imports causes or threatens serious injury to domestic producers. Unlike anti-dumping or countervailing actions, safeguards do not require proof of unfair trade practices. These measures may include increased tariffs, quotas, or other import controls designed to give local industries time to adjust.



## Importance of Trade Remedy Measures

- Protect domestic jobs and industries from unfair competition
- Encourage fair and rules-based international trade
- Prevent market disruption caused by import surges
- Provide local manufacturers time to modernize and compete
- Maintain investor confidence in domestic industries

Without these protections, strategic sectors such as steel, agriculture, manufacturing, and chemicals may suffer long-term damage.



## Trade Remedy Measures in the Philippines

In the Philippines, trade remedy investigations are handled by relevant government agencies such as the Department of Trade and Industry (DTI), Tariff Commission, and Department of Agriculture for agricultural products. Cases may involve imported steel products, cement, chemicals, agricultural goods, and other sensitive sectors.



The country applies these measures in accordance with WTO rules and local laws to ensure fairness while balancing consumer interests and industrial growth. Importers affected by trade remedy measures should consult a [licensed customs broker](/articles/role-of-customs-broker-philippines) to understand how additional duties are computed and how they affect total [importation costs](/articles/importation-costs-breakdown-philippines).
