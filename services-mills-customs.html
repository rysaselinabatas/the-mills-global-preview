What Mills Customs Does

The Mills Customs manages import and export clearance as part of a wider compliance system. The service connects customs filing to importer accreditation, permits, tariff classification, valuation, origin, examinations, taxes, release, records and post-clearance exposure.

See if this sounds like you.

  • PEZA and economic-zone enterprises
  • Foreign companies planning a first Philippine shipment
  • Importers of regulated, high-value or non-routine cargo
  • Companies addressing customs holds, audits or disputes

Core Support

Here's exactly what's included.

01

Import clearance

Review and lodge the applicable customs entry, coordinate assessment, payment, examination and release.

02

Export clearance

Prepare and coordinate export declarations, permits, origin documents and cargo-release formalities.

03

Importer and exporter registration

Support applicable BOC and CPRS registration, accreditation, updating and reportorial requirements.

04

Tariff classification

Review product information to determine or support the appropriate AHTN or HS classification and related permit treatment.

05

Customs valuation

Review transaction data, additions, related-party considerations and supporting documents affecting customs value.

06

Duties and taxes

Prepare estimates and coordinate payment of assessed customs duties, VAT, excise tax and other lawful charges.

07

PEZA and special transactions

Coordinate qualified import, export, transfer, farm-in, farm-out, tax-incentive and ecozone documentation.

08

Examination and hold coordination

Manage documentary response, physical examination, x-ray, sampling and agency coordination as applicable.

09

Post-clearance support

Organize records, conduct readiness reviews and assist with post-clearance audit and compliance concerns.

10

Customs remedies

Coordinate the appropriate professional response to manifest issues, abandonment, seizure, classification, valuation and related disputes.

How It Works

How The Mills Handles The Shipment

01

Review the importer, permits, invoice, packing list, transport document and product information before filing.

02

Confirm the tariff classification, customs value, origin, entry type, taxes and any special requirements.

03

Prepare and lodge the customs declaration through the applicable customs systems and authorized channels.

04

Coordinate assessment, payment, examination, permit verification, cargo release and handover.

05

Complete the shipment file with the customs entry, receipts, permits, release records and any post-clearance requirements.

Why Businesses Choose The Mills

You get a coordinated plan, not disconnected agency instructions.

  • Customs treatment is reviewed before filing, reducing avoidable corrections once the cargo is already under customs processing.
  • Permits, classification, valuation and shipment documents are reviewed as one customs file.
  • Complex, questioned or held shipments are managed through a defined escalation and response process.
  • Post-clearance documentation is organized as part of the shipment, not treated as an afterthought.

Customs assessment, examination, release and enforcement actions remain subject to the Bureau of Customs and other competent authorities. Legal representation and services reserved to licensed professionals are handled through qualified counsel, including Maulit Law where applicable.

Common Questions

Answers before you have to ask.

As of 2026, the Bureau of Customs adopted a three-year validity framework for importer accreditation, subject to current rules, annual or continuing obligations and eligibility. Importers should verify their actual status and applicable renewal process before shipping.

The Mills can review product information and support tariff classification. Where certainty is commercially important, an application for an advance ruling from the Tariff Commission may be considered under the applicable process.

The Mills can organize the customs and documentary file and coordinate the appropriate response. Because seizure and forfeiture are legal proceedings, representation and legal remedies must be handled by duly qualified counsel under a separate engagement.

Release does not end the importer's obligations. The Bureau of Customs may review declarations and records after release, making accurate classification, valuation, permits and recordkeeping essential.
